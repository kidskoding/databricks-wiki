# Total Cost of Ownership

> [!eli5] Summary
> Total cost of ownership is the full cost of a system over its life, including staff time, maintenance, and migration, not only the license or cloud bill.

**In data work:** TCO adds up every cost of owning and running a system over a period, usually three to five years. That includes software and cloud usage, hardware, storage, network [[Egress|egress]], support contracts, staff time to build and maintain it, training, downtime, and the one-time cost of migrating in (and possibly out). Comparing tools on monthly bill alone is misleading: an open-source tool with no license can cost more if it needs a full-time engineer to keep it running. A small retailer comparing platforms should include the hours its data staff spend on patching, upgrades, and failed-job recovery.

**In Databricks:** For Databricks, TCO includes the Databricks usage meter ([[DBUs]]), the cloud infrastructure underneath classic compute, storage, and the people time saved or spent. [[How Databricks Pricing Works]] explains the meters, [[Estimating Costs for a Small Business]] gives a method for estimating, and [[Building a Business Case]] puts TCO next to benefits. Use the official pricing pages rather than quoted figures, since prices change.
