# Proof of Concept

> [!eli5] Summary
> A proof of concept is a short, limited trial that tests whether a tool or approach works for a real problem before a full commitment.

**In data work:** A proof of concept (PoC) is a short project, often two to six weeks, with success criteria agreed in advance, for example: the platform loads the data, runs three key reports faster, and stays within a cost limit. It uses a real but small slice of the problem, not a demo dataset. The output is a decision and evidence, not a finished system. A family-owned distributor might run a PoC that moves one year of sales and one inventory report onto a new platform, then compares speed, effort, and monthly cost with the current setup.

**In Databricks:** A Databricks PoC usually starts on [[Databricks Free Edition and Trials|a trial or Free Edition]], loads a representative table with [[Lakeflow Connect]] or file upload, and rebuilds one or two reports in [[Databricks SQL]]. Track spend from day one with [[Budgets and Usage Dashboards]]. [[Building a Business Case]] and [[Your First 30 Days on Databricks]] explain how to turn PoC results into a decision.
